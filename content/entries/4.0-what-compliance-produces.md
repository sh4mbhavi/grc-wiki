:::lede
Compliance knows which obligations bind the organisation, maps each one to a
control, and keeps the proof that the control ran. Three verbs, four artefacts.
:::

The distinguishing artefact is the mapping. Without it a company running two
frameworks does the same work twice and produces two control inventories that
disagree — which is worse than either alone, because now nobody knows which one
an auditor will be shown.

## The four

:::terms
Obligation register :: Every requirement that binds you, with its source cited — the law, the clause, the contract, the standard. An obligation with no citation cannot be tested and will not survive challenge.
Control inventory :: What you actually do, each control mapped to the obligations it satisfies. See [4.1](/compliance/mapping-obligations-to-controls/).
Evidence store :: The dated proof that each control operated, retrievable by control and by period. See [4.2](/compliance/evidence-that-holds-up/).
Audit response :: The process for receiving a request, producing the evidence, and managing what comes back. See [4.3](/compliance/responding-to-an-audit/).
:::

## Where the obligations come from

Four sources, and programmes routinely forget the third and fourth.

Law and regulation, which is the one everybody starts with. Contracts —
customer agreements with security schedules, supplier terms, insurance
conditions. Standards the organisation has voluntarily committed to, which bind
in practice once a certificate is on the website. And internal policy, which
binds because you wrote it down and told people to follow it.

The contractual set is usually the largest and the least catalogued. A company
with two hundred customer contracts has agreed to a lot of specific things and
frequently cannot list them.

## The mapping is the leverage

One quarterly access review can satisfy an ISO 27001 Annex A control, a SOC 2
criterion, a customer contractual clause and an internal policy — with the same
evidence, produced once.

That only happens if something records the relationship. An inventory with no
mapping column is a to-do list; the mapping is what turns a control programme
into a compliance programme.

:::def term="Obligation" ref="4.1"
A requirement binding the organisation, from law, contract, standard or its own
policy, recorded with the source that creates it.
:::

## Compliance is not the same as being safe

Worth stating plainly, because the conflation causes real harm in both
directions. A compliant organisation has met a defined set of obligations. Those
obligations were written by somebody else, against a general case, at a point in
the past.

They are a floor. The risk function exists to find what the floor does not
cover, and a compliance programme that has quietly replaced the risk register
with a control checklist has removed the only part of the function that was
looking at your specific situation.
