:::lede
Appetite is the amount of risk an organisation is *willing to accept* in
pursuit of its objectives. Tolerance is the *acceptable variation* around a
particular objective. Appetite is a stance; tolerance is a boundary on a number.
:::

The two are used interchangeably in conversation and mean different things in
every framework that defines them. The distinction matters at exactly one moment:
when someone has to decide whether a specific risk gets treated or accepted.

## The difference, stated plainly

**Appetite** is set once a year, by the board, and covers a category. *We accept
moderate operational risk to enter new markets quickly; we accept no risk that
would compromise customer funds.* It is directional and mostly qualitative.

**Tolerance** is the numeric edge on a specific objective. *Availability must not
fall below 99.5% in any calendar month.* Cross it and something specific happens
- an escalation, a treatment, a board paper.

Appetite tells you which risks you are in the business of taking. Tolerance tells
you when a particular one has gone too far.

## Why appetite statements usually fail

Most published appetite statements cannot be applied to a decision, which is the
only test that matters. The three ways they fail:

**Too abstract to test.** "We have a low appetite for cyber risk" cannot resolve
a specific question. Against what score, in what category, approved by whom?

**No threshold.** An appetite with no number attached cannot tell you whether the
risk in front of you is inside it. The usable form names a residual score - *no
accepted risk above 12 without board sign-off* - even when the category framing
stays qualitative.

**Uniform across categories.** An organisation genuinely has different appetites
for different things. A single statement covering all risk is either so
permissive it permits everything or so restrictive it is ignored.

:::def term="Risk capacity" ref="3.4"
The maximum risk the organisation could absorb before it becomes unable to
operate. Appetite is set below capacity; the gap is deliberate.
:::

## How they are used together

The risk function scores an entry, produces a residual figure, and compares it
against the appetite threshold for that category. Above it, the entry needs a
treatment plan or a documented acceptance at the right level of authority. Below
it, the entry is monitored on its review cycle.

Tolerance operates in a different loop, usually automated: a metric crosses a
line, someone is notified, an issue is raised. It is the operational tripwire;
appetite is the annual stance the tripwire was placed to protect.

Get the two the wrong way round - an appetite reviewed monthly, a tolerance
argued about annually - and the function spends its time on the wrong meeting.
