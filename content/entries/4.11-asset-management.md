:::lede
Asset management is the **maintained, owned inventory of everything the
organisation runs** — hardware, software, data stores, cloud services and the
business services built on them — kept current enough that other controls can
rely on it.
:::

It is listed first in the CIS Controls and near the front of ISO 27001's Annex A
for one reason: every control that follows takes the asset list as input. You
cannot patch what you do not know you run, grant least-privilege access to a
system nobody recorded, back up an undocumented database or scope an audit
against an inventory that is wrong. A gap in the asset register is a gap in
every downstream control at once.

The hard part is not building the list. It is keeping it true. An inventory is a
control only while it reflects reality this week; a spreadsheet last reconciled
at the previous audit is a document, not a control.

## What counts as an asset

Wider than the hardware most people picture.

:::terms
Hardware :: Servers, laptops, mobile devices, network kit, and anything with an IP address on the corporate network — including the devices nobody provisioned.
Software :: Operating systems, applications, libraries and firmware, each with a version, so a new CVE can be matched to what is actually installed.
Data :: Repositories and their classification — where regulated or sensitive data lives, which is what a DLP or access-review programme scopes against.
Cloud services :: Accounts, subscriptions, SaaS tenancies and the resources inside them. The fastest-growing category and the one least often fully captured.
Business services :: The customer-facing or revenue-bearing services that sit on top, mapped to the technical assets they depend on, so an outage or a risk can be described in business terms.
:::

:::def term="Asset owner" ref="4.1"
The named individual accountable for an asset's classification, its acceptable
use and the risk decisions about it. Not the person who administers it day to
day — the person who answers for it. An asset with no owner is the one that goes
unpatched.
:::

## Inventory and CMDB are not the same thing

A configuration management database records *relationships* — this service runs
on those hosts, which depend on that database — and supports change and incident
management. An asset inventory records *existence, ownership and classification*
and supports risk and compliance. Mature organisations reconcile the two; small
ones should not try to run a full CMDB before the basic inventory is reliable.

## Where asset management fails

**One-time discovery.** A scan is run, the results are pasted into a sheet, and
nothing reconciles it afterwards. Cloud resources and personal devices appear
faster than the annual refresh catches them, so accuracy decays from the day the
snapshot is taken.

**No owner field, or a team name in it.** "IT" owns 400 assets, which means
nobody does. When a vulnerability lands, there is no individual to task and the
remediation clock does not start.

**Shadow IT uncounted.** A department buys a SaaS tool on a card. It holds
customer data, it is in nobody's inventory, and it surfaces for the first time
during a breach or a due-diligence questionnaire.

**Classification missing.** The list has every host but does not say which ones
hold regulated data, so every downstream control has to treat all assets alike
and effort cannot be prioritised.

## Common questions

:::faq
### What is asset management in cyber security?

The practice of keeping an accurate, owned inventory of all hardware, software,
data and services the organisation uses, so that security controls — patching,
access control, monitoring, backup, incident response — can be applied
completely. It is the foundational control: everything else assumes the list is
right.

### What is the difference between an asset inventory and a CMDB?

An asset inventory answers "what do we have, who owns it, how sensitive is it".
A CMDB (configuration management database) answers "how are these things
connected and what changed". The inventory serves risk and compliance; the CMDB
serves change and incident management. They overlap but are built for different
questions.

### How often should the asset inventory be updated?

Continuously for anything that can be discovered automatically — network scans,
cloud APIs, endpoint agents feeding the inventory in near real time — with a
periodic manual reconciliation, at least quarterly, for the categories that
cannot be discovered, such as third-party SaaS and data classification.

### Who is the asset owner?

The named person accountable for decisions about an asset: its classification,
who may use it, and whether an identified risk to it is accepted or treated. It
is a business-accountability role, not the system administrator who operates it.
:::
