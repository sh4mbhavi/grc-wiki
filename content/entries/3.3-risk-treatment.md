:::lede
There are four treatments and no others. Accept it, reduce it, move it to
somebody else, or stop doing the thing that creates it.
:::

Every entry above appetite carries one of them, with an owner and a date. The
value of the closed list is that it forces a decision: a risk with no treatment
selected has not been decided about, however much has been written under it.

## The four

:::terms
Accept :: Take the risk as it stands. Requires an approval at the right level of authority, recorded with a date and a review point. Acceptance is a decision, not the absence of one.
Mitigate :: Add or strengthen a control so the residual score falls. Requires a target score, a control that will exist, an owner and a date. Most treatments are this.
Transfer :: Move the financial consequence elsewhere: insurance, a contractual indemnity, an outsourced service. Note what does not transfer: the regulatory obligation and the reputational consequence almost never do.
Avoid :: Stop the activity. Rare, and usually the right answer exactly once, when the activity was never worth its risk.
:::

## Why "monitor" is not on the list

It is the most common entry in a treatment column and it is the absence of a
treatment, written to avoid the acceptance decision somebody would have to sign.

Everything is monitored; that is what the review cycle is. Writing "monitor"
says the risk is being accepted without anybody putting their name to the
acceptance, which is precisely the situation the register exists to prevent.

If the answer is genuinely accept, write accept, and get it approved. It is a
respectable answer and auditors treat it as one.

## What each treatment has to evidence

:::attrs a="At selection" b="At review"
Accept :: Approval at the right authority, dated :: Still inside appetite, or re-decided
Mitigate :: Target score, named control, owner, date :: Control exists and is operating
Transfer :: The contract or policy, and what it excludes :: Cover still in force, limits still adequate
Avoid :: Decision record, and confirmation the activity stopped :: No quiet resumption
:::

The right-hand column is the one programmes skip. A mitigation approved eighteen
months ago, whose control was never built, still shows a reduced residual score
in most registers, which makes the register actively misleading rather than
merely stale.

:::def term="Target residual" ref="3.1"
The score a mitigation is expected to reach, stated when the treatment is
chosen. Without it there is no test for whether the treatment worked.
:::

## Sequencing when everything is above appetite

A new programme frequently finds twenty entries above the threshold and capacity
for four. The instinct is to lower the appetite until the list fits, which
destroys the artefact.

The honest move is to treat four, accept the rest explicitly with a named
approver and a shorter review cycle, and put the acceptance list in front of
governance. That converts a resourcing problem into a decision the organisation
has actually made, which is both defensible in audit and considerably more
likely to release budget.
