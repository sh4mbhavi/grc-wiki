:::lede
The statement of applicability records which Annex A controls apply to your
ISMS, which do not, and the justification for each decision. ISO/IEC 27001
clause 6.1.3 d) requires it, and the auditor reads it before anything else.
:::

It is a decision record, not a control inventory. That distinction is where most
first attempts go wrong: a document listing 93 controls with "implemented" beside
each says nothing about why any of them applies to you.

## What each row needs

:::terms
Control reference :: The Annex A identifier, from the 2022 revision's 93 controls across four themes: organisational, people, physical, technological.
Applicable :: Yes or no. Not "partially": a control either applies to your scope or it does not.
Justification :: Why. For an inclusion this is usually the risk it treats or the obligation it meets; for an exclusion it is why the scope does not reach it.
Implementation status :: Whether it is in place, and where the detail lives, a pointer to your own control inventory rather than a description.
:::

The justification column is the one that gets skimmed and the one that is read
hardest. "Applicable, best practice" is not a justification. "Applicable, treats
R-014, remote access to production" is.

## Exclusions

Excluding a control is legitimate and expected. A company with no physical
premises excludes several physical controls; a company that writes no software
excludes secure development controls.

What is not legitimate is excluding a control because it is inconvenient. The
justification is tested, and the obvious tell is an exclusion that contradicts
the scope statement: no development controls, in a scope covering a product the
company builds.

Where an exclusion is genuine, the phrasing that survives is the one that ties
back to scope rather than to circumstance: not "we do not currently do this",
but "this activity is outside the ISMS scope defined in clause 4.3".

## How it relates to your other artefacts

The SoA is the bridge between the risk assessment and the control inventory. The
risk assessment says what needs treating; the SoA says which Annex A controls
are in play as a result; the inventory says what you actually do and who owns it.

Keeping them as three artefacts is correct. Merging the SoA into the inventory
produces a document that is neither a decision record nor an operating list, and
which has to be untangled at the next audit.

:::def term="ISMS scope" ref="5.0"
The boundary of the management system, stated under clause 4.3. Everything in
the SoA is a decision made relative to it, which is why an exclusion that
contradicts scope fails immediately.
:::

## Keeping it current

Review it whenever the scope changes, whenever the risk assessment produces a
new treatment, and at the annual management review. A SoA carrying last year's
scope is the most common documentation finding in surveillance audits, and it is
entirely avoidable.
