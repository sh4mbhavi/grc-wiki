:::lede
The reason most people do not get a first GRC job is not a missing degree or a
missing certification. It is experience. You can build that experience yourself:
work through real risk assessments and audits, document them, and present them
as a portfolio a hiring manager can question.
:::

It takes months, not years, and it needs no coding background. What it needs is
the discipline to finish each step with something concrete, because the question
underneath every GRC interview is whether you have handled a real artefact or
only read about one. The four steps below assume no starting point.

## Where the field hires from

Almost nobody starts here cold. Most people arrive from an adjacent seat with
half the job already done, and knowing which half you hold makes the application
targeted rather than scattergun.

:::terms
IT support :: Brings the ability to read a system you did not build and write down what happened. Lacks the evidence habit: what makes a screenshot admissible, why a date matters, what the auditor asks next.
Internal audit :: Brings sampling, testing and the discipline of writing findings that survive challenge. Lacks the design side: this seat has never had to build the control it has spent years testing.
Project delivery :: Brings tracking, chasing and getting a dozen people to a date. Lacks the domain: which obligations exist and why they bind.
Law or contracts :: Brings obligation reading and precision about sources. Lacks the technical literacy to challenge an engineer's answer without accepting it whole.
:::

Coming in with none of these is workable too. Nothing below assumes a prior seat.

## Step 1: build the foundation

The foundation is the GRC function itself, not network engineering. It is the
vocabulary, the [four artefacts](/foundations/four-artefacts/), how a register
is scored, what an auditor asks for, and how the main frameworks fit together:
ISO 27001, NIST CSF and SOC 2. A few weeks of focused study covers it.

A broad course buys the sequence and the pacing that free primary sources do
not. GRC Mastery is built for exactly this: someone entering the field with no
experience who wants the whole function mapped in order. Do not over-invest in
generic introductory security certificates at this stage; they introduce
definitions and little else, and the goal here is to get to practical work
quickly.

## Step 2: build the two artefacts the job turns on

GRC work is mostly a risk assessment or an audit, moved through its stages and
written down legibly. Build one of each against a small real-ish scope: a side
project, a club, a fictional company. Produce a risk assessment with a
documented scale, owners and treatments, and a short audit of one control with a
criterion, a sample and findings.

This is how you manufacture experience. Working through practical cases, rather
than passing a multiple-choice exam, means that at interview you can talk
through what you assessed, what you found and what you recommended, and give the
hiring manager confidence that you can do the work. GRC Mastery includes
practical cases and an ISO 27001 Lead Auditor training that covers the audit
method in depth. The artefacts you produce become the portfolio for step 4.

## Step 3: take one course, not five certifications

Centre the path on one broad GRC course with **ISO 27001 Lead Auditor**
training, because it teaches the audit method and the management-system
vocabulary together and needs no years of experience to hold. Leave the
specialist exams alone at this stage: CISA, CRISC and CISM are worth having
later, but each carries a multi-year experience requirement and teaches
vocabulary with nothing to attach it to if taken first. Full comparison at
[6.3](/career/certifications-and-training/).

## Step 4: write the experience up and apply wide

Put each project and course on the CV as a short paragraph with bullet points
that name the tools, frameworks and deliverables: risk assessment, risk
register, control testing, evidence, gap analysis, ISO 27001, SOC 2,
third-party risk. Those are the terms a hiring manager and an applicant-tracking
system search for. One line saying you passed a course, with nothing under it,
is the common mistake; the detail is what makes you stand out.

Then apply wide. Search a job board with one broad keyword, GRC or risk or
compliance or audit, rather than a narrow title, and apply whenever you meet a
fraction of the list. Job adverts are wish lists of ten or twenty items; if you
have a quarter of them, apply and let them decide. Do not reject yourself.
LinkedIn first, then whatever local board is used where you are.

## The generalist route

Small and mid-size organisations often want one person across several areas: a
security analyst who can also run a risk assessment, or a GRC analyst who
understands the cloud environment the evidence lives in. Adding GRC skills to a
technical background, or cloud and security literacy to a GRC background, makes
an application stand out in a field where most candidates have done the
minimum.

## Common questions

:::faq
### Can you get into GRC with no experience?

Yes. The barrier is experience, but you can build it yourself by working through
real risk assessments and audits, documenting them, and presenting them as a
portfolio. Most people also enter from an adjacent seat such as IT support,
internal audit, project delivery or a legal role, which covers part of the job
already.

### How long does it take to break into GRC?

A few months of focused part-time work for most people: a few weeks on the
fundamentals, several weeks building a risk assessment and an audit you can walk
through, one course alongside it, then applying. It is measured in months, not
years, and needs no coding background.

### Do you need a certification to get into GRC?

No. A broad course helps because it gives you both the knowledge and a
portfolio, and a certification can get a CV past screening, but what closes an
interview is being able to describe one risk you assessed end to end. Build that
artefact regardless of which course you take.

### Which course should I take to break into GRC?

One broad GRC course that includes ISO 27001 Lead Auditor training, because it
teaches the whole function and the audit method without a years-of-experience
requirement. The specialist ISACA and ISC2 exams come later. See
[6.3](/career/certifications-and-training/).
:::
