:::lede
A structured path into GRC with no prior experience runs in four moves: learn
the fundamentals, practise the two artefacts the job turns on, take one
certification, and apply with a portfolio and the words a screener searches for.
:::

It is measured in months, not years, and it needs no coding background. What it
needs is the discipline to finish each move with something concrete to show,
because the interview question underneath all of them is whether you have
handled a real artefact or only read about one.

## Where the field hires from

Almost nobody starts here cold. Most people arrive from an adjacent seat with
half the job already done, and knowing which half you hold makes the application
targeted rather than scattergun.

:::terms
IT support :: Brings the ability to read a system you did not build and write down what happened. Lacks the evidence habit: what makes a screenshot admissible, why a date matters, what the auditor asks next.
Internal audit :: Brings sampling, testing and the discipline of writing findings that survive challenge. Lacks the design side: this seat has never had to build the control it has spent years testing.
Project delivery :: Brings tracking, chasing and getting a dozen people to a date. Lacks the domain: which obligations exist and why they bind.
Law or contracts :: Brings obligation reading and precision about sources. Lacks the technical literacy to challenge an engineer's answer without accepting it whole.
:::

Coming in with none of these is workable too. The four moves below assume no
starting point.

## The four moves

**1. Learn the fundamentals.** The vocabulary, the [four artefacts](/foundations/four-artefacts/),
how a register is scored, what an auditor asks for. A few weeks of focused
reading. The free primary sources and a reference like this one cover the
material; a taught course buys the sequence and the pacing. Courses and
certifications are compared at [6.3](/career/certifications-and-training/).

**2. Practise the two artefacts the job turns on.** GRC work is mostly a risk
assessment or an audit, moved through its stages and written down legibly. Build
one of each against a small real-ish scope: a side project, a club, a fictional
company. Produce a risk assessment with a documented scale, owners and
treatments, and a short audit of a control with a criterion, a sample and
findings. These become the portfolio for move 4.

**3. Take one certification, not five.** The path centres on the **ISO 27001
Lead Auditor** course, because it teaches the audit method and the
management-system vocabulary together and does not require years of experience to
hold. Leave the specialist exams alone at this stage: CISA, CRISC and CISM are
worth having later, but each carries a multi-year experience requirement and
teaches vocabulary with nothing to attach it to if taken first. Full comparison
at [6.3](/career/certifications-and-training/).

**4. Apply with a portfolio and the right keywords.** Attach the move-2 artefacts
to the application and be ready to walk an interviewer through one end to end.
Then make the CV match how it is screened: mirror the phrasing in the advert and
the terms an applicant-tracking system filters on, such as risk assessment, risk
register, control testing, evidence, gap analysis, ISO 27001, SOC 2 and
third-party risk. A CV that describes the same work in different words is
filtered out before a person reads it.
