:::lede
Almost nobody starts here. The field hires from IT support, internal audit,
project delivery and law, and each route arrives with most of one half of the
job already done.
:::

Knowing which half you already have is the difference between a targeted
application and a scattergun one.

## What each route brings, and what it lacks

:::terms
IT support :: Brings the ability to read a system you did not build and write down what happened. Lacks the evidence habit — what makes a screenshot admissible, why a date matters, what the auditor asks next.
Internal audit :: Brings sampling, testing and the discipline of writing findings that survive challenge. Lacks the design side: this seat has never had to build the control it has spent years testing.
Project delivery :: Brings tracking, chasing and getting a dozen people to a date. Lacks the domain — which obligations exist and why they bind.
Law or contracts :: Brings obligation reading and precision about sources. Lacks the technical literacy to challenge an engineer's answer without accepting it whole.
:::

## The thing that beats a certificate

One artefact you built, that you can walk somebody through end to end.

A risk you assessed: how it was raised, how you scored it and against what
scale, who owned it, what treatment was chosen, who approved it, what evidence
closed it. Ten minutes, specific, with the parts that went wrong included.

A candidate who can do that outperforms one with three certificates and nothing
to describe, every time, because the interviewer's actual question is whether
you have handled a real artefact or only read about them.

If your current job has none, build one. Assess a risk in your own team's work,
properly, with a documented scale. It is a real artefact even if the
organisation never asked for it.

## Certificates, honestly

They help with the screening filter and they do not close the interview.

CISA is the most widely recognised in audit-adjacent roles and does not assume a
technical background. ISO 27001 Lead Implementer teaches the management-system
vocabulary faster than anything else. CRISC maps well to the risk seat. A
security certification like Security+ helps only if the gap you are closing is
technical literacy.

Sequencing matters more than choice: a certificate taken before you have handled
an artefact teaches you vocabulary with nothing to attach it to, and it fades.

## The gap that actually blocks people

Not technical depth. The evidence habit — understanding that the job is not
doing the thing but being able to show, months later, to somebody who was not
there, that the thing was done.

It is learnable in months and it is the thing hiring managers are listening for.
Candidates who talk about what they did are common; candidates who talk about
what they kept are not.
