:::lede
ISO/IEC 27001 is the international standard for an **information security
management system** — a documented, risk-driven way of running security that an
accredited body can audit and certify. It certifies the *system*, not any
product, team or piece of software.
:::

The distinction between the management system and the controls is the thing to
hold onto. Clauses 4 to 10 are the requirements: they describe the machine —
scope, leadership, risk assessment, objectives, internal audit, management
review, corrective action. Annex A is a reference list of 93 controls the risk
assessment can draw on. You are certified against the clauses. Annex A is where
you shop, guided by your risks, and your choices are recorded in the
[Statement of Applicability](/compliance/statement-of-applicability/).

A certificate says an accredited auditor examined that machine and found it
running: risks are being assessed, controls are being chosen and operated, and
the organisation is finding and fixing its own failures. It does not say the
organisation cannot be breached.

## The requirement clauses

:::terms
4 — Context :: Define the scope: which parts of the organisation, which locations, which services. Identify interested parties and their requirements. A scope drawn too wide is unauditable; drawn too narrow it is worthless to a customer.
5 — Leadership :: Top management owns the ISMS, sets the policy, and assigns roles. "Management commitment" is audited by evidence — attendance at reviews, resourcing decisions — not by a signed statement.
6 — Planning :: The risk assessment methodology, the risk assessment itself, the risk treatment plan, and measurable security objectives. The 2022 version also requires that changes to the ISMS be planned.
7 — Support :: Resources, competence, awareness, communication, and control of documented information.
8 — Operation :: Actually running the risk assessment and treatment on the planned cadence and whenever something significant changes.
9 — Performance evaluation :: Monitoring and measurement, internal audit, and management review. This is where most first-time certifications struggle, because it requires records built up over months.
10 — Improvement :: Handling nonconformities with corrective action, and continually improving the ISMS.
:::

:::def term="Statement of Applicability" ref="5.3"
The document listing every Annex A control, whether it applies, and the
justification for each inclusion and exclusion. Required by clause 6.1.3 d) and
the first artefact an auditor asks for.
:::

## Annex A, 2022 revision

The 2022 update cut the control count from 114 to 93 and reorganised them from 14
domains into four themes: **organisational** (37), **people** (8), **physical**
(14) and **technological** (34). Eleven controls are genuinely new, including
threat intelligence, information security for cloud services, ICT readiness for
business continuity, data masking, data leakage prevention, web filtering and
secure coding. Organisations certified against the 2013 version had a transition
window that closed on 31 October 2025.

A 2024 amendment added climate-change consideration to clauses 4.1 and 4.2 — a
small wording change, but auditors now expect to see that climate has been
considered as a contextual issue.

## The certification path

1. **Gap analysis** — measure the current state against the clauses and Annex A.
2. **Implementation** — build the missing management-system machinery and
   controls. Typically 6–12 months for an organisation starting from little.
3. **Operate for evidence** — run the ISMS long enough to have real records: at
   least one internal audit, one management review, one cycle of the risk
   assessment. Usually a minimum of three months.
4. **Stage 1 audit** — the certification body reviews documentation and
   readiness.
5. **Stage 2 audit** — the certification body tests the ISMS in operation. A
   pass, possibly with minor nonconformities to close, leads to the certificate.
6. **Surveillance** — smaller audits in years one and two, a full
   recertification in year three.

Choose a certification body accredited under ISO/IEC 27006 by a recognised
national accreditation body. An unaccredited certificate is not recognised by
procurement and is a common false economy.

## Where ISO 27001 goes wrong

**Buying Annex A, ignoring the clauses.** A control set is implemented, the
management system around it is not, and Stage 2 fails on clause 9 because there
are no internal audit or management-review records.

**Scope drawn to flatter the certificate.** A narrow scope — one product, one
office — gets the certificate cheaply, then a customer reads the scope statement
and finds their service is not in it.

**Risk assessment as a formality.** A generic risk register is produced to
satisfy clause 6, disconnected from the SoA, so control choices cannot be traced
to any risk.

**Certificate treated as the finish line.** The ISMS goes quiet after the audit,
surveillance year one finds it dormant, and the finding is a major nonconformity.

## Common questions

:::faq
### What is ISO 27001 in simple terms?

An international standard that sets out how to run information security as a
managed, repeatable system: decide what you are protecting, assess the risks,
choose and operate controls, check that it works, and fix what does not. An
accredited auditor can then certify that the system is genuinely in place.

### What is the difference between ISO 27001 and ISO 27002?

ISO 27001 is the certifiable standard — its clauses are the requirements you are
audited against. ISO 27002 is a companion guidance document that explains how to
implement each Annex A control in detail. You certify against 27001; you read
27002 for implementation advice. You cannot be certified against 27002.

### How long does ISO 27001 certification take?

For an organisation starting with few formal controls, typically 9 to 18 months:
several months to build the management system and controls, a minimum operating
period of about three months to generate records, then the two-stage audit.
Organisations with mature controls already in place can be faster.

### What changed in ISO 27001:2022?

The requirement clauses (4–10) changed only slightly. Annex A was restructured
from 114 controls in 14 domains to 93 controls in 4 themes, with 11 new controls
covering areas such as cloud services, threat intelligence, secure coding and
data leakage prevention. A 2024 amendment added climate change as a contextual
consideration.

### Does ISO 27001 certification mean a company is secure?

No. It means an accredited auditor found a working information security
management system — risks assessed, controls chosen and operated, failures found
and corrected. It is strong evidence of a disciplined approach, not a guarantee
against a breach.
:::
