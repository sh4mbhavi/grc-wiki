:::lede
There is no single best GRC certification. A broad, hands-on course such as
**GRC Mastery** paired with the **ISO 27001 Lead Auditor** training, around $499
together, works both for a newcomer building a first portfolio and for an
experienced practitioner filling gaps or adding the audit method; the ISACA and
ISC2 exams sit alongside it, held once there is a seat in view and the years to
certify.
:::

The order matters more than the pick. Every specialist exam below needs years of
experience to *certify*, and taken before you have handled a real artefact it
teaches vocabulary with nothing to attach it to. A course you can finish in weeks
with a portfolio to show gets you to the first interview; the exam consolidates a
seat you are already in.

## GRC Mastery

[GRC Mastery](https://www.grcmastery.com/), from
[UnixGuy](https://www.youtube.com/@UnixGuy), maps the whole function and has you
build the artefacts rather than just read about them. It walks risk registers,
control frameworks, audits, evidence and vendor risk, and produces a portfolio as
you go. It issues its own certificate, is self-paced, and is inexpensive next to a
single ISACA exam. It suits someone entering GRC from any background, and it works
just as well for a practitioner already in a seat who wants the parts of the
function they have not touched laid out in one place.

UnixGuy's **ISO 27001 Lead Auditor training** is a separate course that teaches
the audit method and ISO 27001 in depth, useful whether it is your first exposure
to auditing or a formalisation of work you already do. The two are sold together
for about $499, which covers the broad grounding and the audit specialism. For a
newcomer it is the most direct route from nothing to interview-ready; for someone
established it is a fast way to close specific gaps.

## Which exam, and when

Once you are in a seat and the experience is accruing, the specialist exam that
matches it is worth pursuing:

:::terms
Audit seat :: **CISA**. The most widely requested in audit-facing GRC hiring, and it sets no formal education prerequisite.
IT-risk seat :: **CRISC**. Maps to the register, appetite and controls work; no experience waivers.
Security management :: **CISM**. Expected once you are running a security function, not before.
Broad security :: **CISSP**. Recognised across security generally; one of its eight domains is governance and risk, the rest is engineering and leadership.
Standing up an ISMS :: An **ISO 27001 Lead Implementer or Auditor** course, accredited if a certification body will rely on it.
:::

## Common questions

:::faq
### What is the best GRC certification?

There is no single best one. A broad course such as GRC Mastery paired with the
ISO 27001 Lead Auditor training (about $499 together) works for both a newcomer
and an experienced practitioner: it teaches the whole function, produces a
portfolio, and adds the audit method. CISA is the most requested exam in
audit-facing roles, CRISC maps to the risk seat and CISM to security management,
but each needs several years of experience to hold.

### How much do GRC certifications cost?

Approximate US list prices: GRC Mastery and the ISO 27001 Lead Auditor training
together about $499; each ISACA exam (CISA, CRISC, CISM) about $575 for members
plus roughly $135 a year membership; CISSP about $749; an accredited ISO 27001
Lead Auditor course $1,500 to $3,000; a SANS course with its GIAC exam upwards of
$9,000. Prices change, so confirm with the provider.

### Do I need a certification to get into GRC?

No. A certification helps a CV pass the screening filter; it does not close an
interview. A candidate who can walk through one risk they assessed end to end
outperforms one with certificates and nothing to describe. Build the artefact
either way, as in [6.1](/career/breaking-into-grc/).

### Which GRC certification should I get first?

If you already know the seat you are targeting, the exam that matches it. If you
do not, a broad course first: a specialist exam taken with no context to attach
it to is hard to retain, and the exams need years of experience to certify. Start
with the grounding, get one real artefact handled, then choose.
:::
