:::lede
Continuous control monitoring is **automated, ongoing checking that a control is
operating**, pulling the signal straight from the system rather than
screenshotting it once a quarter. Continuous compliance is the broader goal it
feeds — staying provably within requirements over time — which takes acting on
what the monitoring finds, not watching alone.
:::

It closes the gap between "we do this" and "we can show we did this" to near zero
for the controls it covers, because the evidence [4.2](/compliance/evidence-that-holds-up/)
is produced at the moment the control operates rather than gathered in the four
weeks before fieldwork. What it cannot do is exercise judgement, so it earns its
place on the controls a machine can check and leaves the rest exactly where they
were.

## What it changes about evidence

Point-in-time evidence answers one question: did the control run on the day
someone looked. Continuous control monitoring answers a harder one: has it run
every day since, and can you see the moment it stopped. The record is pulled at
the point of operation, continuously, from the system that enforces the control
rather than assembled by hand before an audit.

That is the whole value. Evidence [4.2](/compliance/evidence-that-holds-up/)
collected this way is dated and attributable by construction, and complete for the
period its coverage actually spans — provided the monitoring itself has no gaps and
its telemetry can be trusted. The programme stops sampling its own best month and
starts holding the full population it covers.

## What automation suits, and what it does not

Automation suits a control whose correct state a machine can read without
interpretation. It does not suit a control whose correctness is a person's
judgement, and wiring one of those to a green tick produces a light with no
thought behind it.

| Suits continuous monitoring | Does not |
| --- | --- |
| MFA coverage across privileged accounts | A manager's review of an exception |
| Encryption configuration on a data store | A risk-acceptance decision |
| Access still matching the joiner-mover-leaver record | Whether a vendor is trustworthy |
| Patch currency against a baseline | Whether a policy is the right policy |

The left column is machine-checkable: there is a correct state and the system can
report the current one against it. The right column is not. A tool can confirm
that a review was recorded; it cannot confirm the review was any good, and
treating the record of a judgement as the judgement itself is how a green
dashboard hides a hollow control.

## What it feeds, and what it does not replace

A continuously monitored control is a natural source for a leading indicator.
Coverage trending down — privileged accounts without a second factor creeping
from two per cent to four — is an early warning while there is still time to act,
not a surprise discovered at quarter end.

:::def term="Key risk indicator" ref="3.11"
A leading metric that warns exposure is moving toward appetite before it becomes
an incident. A continuously monitored control is one of the cleanest feeds for
one, because the coverage figure updates itself.
:::

It does not replace the audit. An auditor still tests, still selects the sample
and still concludes independently — but tests against a population that is
already instrumented, where the evidence exists for every instance rather than
the ones that were remembered. Continuous monitoring makes fieldwork shorter and
duller. It does not make it unnecessary, and a programme that treats a platform's
green dashboard as an audit opinion has confused the two.

## Where it fails

**Watching the tool, not the control.** A monitor that confirms the scanner is
online says nothing about whether the scan is finding anything. The signal has to
be the operation of the control, not the availability of the thing that checks
it.

**Alerting on everything.** A monitor that fires on every deviation, at any
severity, trains everyone to ignore it. Within a month the alerts are a filtered
folder and a real breach sits in it unread. Thresholds and routing are part of
the design, not an afterthought.

**"Continuous" that is really monthly.** A check that runs on a schedule slow
enough to miss the thing it watches is point-in-time sampling with a dashboard.
If a control can drift and be restored between runs, the gap is invisible and the
word is marketing.

**Coverage mistaken for effectiveness.** Buying a compliance-automation platform
and counting the controls it touches measures reach, not operation. A control can
be monitored, green and not working; coverage is the count of controls watched,
effectiveness is whether the watched control does its job.

## Common questions

:::faq
### What is continuous control monitoring?
It is automated, ongoing checking that a control is operating, taken from the
system that runs the control rather than reconstructed by hand before an audit.
Instead of confirming a control worked on the day someone screenshotted it, it
confirms the control has stayed in its required state and shows the moment it did
not. It suits controls with a machine-checkable state — MFA coverage, encryption
settings, patch currency [4.10](/compliance/vulnerability-management/) — and is
poorly suited to controls that turn on human judgement.

### What is the difference between continuous monitoring and continuous compliance?
Continuous monitoring is one activity inside continuous compliance, not the whole
of it. Monitoring reads a control's condition on an ongoing basis. Continuous
compliance is the broader practice of staying provably within requirements over
time — which takes evaluating what the monitoring surfaces and remediating what it
finds, not monitoring alone. Monitoring makes the state visible; holding it is
more than watching.

### Can continuous monitoring replace an audit?
No. It feeds an audit rather than removing the need for one. An auditor still
selects a sample, tests independently and forms an opinion, but does it against a
population that is already instrumented, so fieldwork is shorter and holds fewer
surprises. A platform's green dashboard is evidence for an audit, not a
substitute for the auditor's own testing.
:::
