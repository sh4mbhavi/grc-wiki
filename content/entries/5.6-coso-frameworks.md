:::lede
A **COSO framework** is one of two distinct reference models published by the Committee of Sponsoring Organizations of the Treadway Commission. The two are the Internal Control - Integrated Framework (2013) and Enterprise Risk Management - Integrating with Strategy and Performance (2017), routinely confused as though they were a single document.
:::

Because both carry the COSO name, a requirement, a contract clause or an audit request that cites "the COSO framework" without a year is ambiguous - the two answer different questions for different audiences. Neither is certifiable. Both are reference models an organisation adopts and assesses itself against, not a bar an accredited body signs off.

## The two frameworks

Both are built from five components, which is part of why they are mixed up. The components are different sets doing different work.

:::terms
Internal Control - Integrated Framework (2013) :: The model management uses to assess internal control. Five components - control environment; risk assessment; control activities; information and communication; and monitoring activities - sit above 17 principles. It underpins internal control over financial reporting (ICFR) for SOX section 404, and its five components are what the SOC 2 Common Criteria (CC1-CC5) are organised around.
Enterprise Risk Management - Integrating with Strategy and Performance (2017) :: The model for governing risk across the enterprise. Five components - governance and culture; strategy and objective-setting; performance; review and revision; and information, communication and reporting - sit above 20 principles. It is broader than internal control, tying risk-taking to strategy and performance rather than to the soundness of a control set.
:::

## Internal control against enterprise risk

The two are often set side by side, so the distinction is worth stating plainly.

| Dimension | Internal Control - Integrated Framework (2013) | Enterprise Risk Management (2017) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Purpose | Whether internal control, especially over financial reporting, is designed and operating soundly | How the whole enterprise takes and manages risk against its strategy and objectives |
| Structure | Five components, 17 principles | Five components, 20 principles |
| Typical use | SOX section 404, external financial audit, SOC 2 Common Criteria | Board risk oversight, risk appetite, strategy-linked risk |

The one-line test is what each answers. The 2013 framework answers whether internal control is sound; the 2017 framework answers how the enterprise takes risk against its strategy. Internal control is a component of good risk management, not the whole of it, which is why ERM is the wider model and ICIF the narrower discipline it partly contains.

:::def term="Control model" ref="5.0"
A published reference framework an organisation adopts to structure and assess its own control or risk, rather than a standard an accredited body certifies it against. COSO's Internal Control - Integrated Framework serves internal control; COSO ERM serves enterprise risk. Neither certifies an organisation.
:::

## Where each shows up

Which framework is in play is settled by the audience, not by preference.

**Financial reporting and control attestation.** SOX section 404 requires management to assess ICFR against a recognised control framework, and does not name COSO. In practice COSO's Internal Control - Integrated Framework is the near-universal choice among US issuers, to the point that "COSO" in a SOX context almost always means the 2013 internal control model. The SOC 2 Common Criteria (CC1-CC5) align to its five components, so a SOC 2 examination rests on ICIF whether or not the report says so.

**Board-level risk governance.** Risk appetite, strategy-linked risk and board-level oversight - the province of [governance](/governance/what-governance-produces/) - sit in COSO ERM. It is the United States and COSO-world counterpart to ISO 31000, the international risk-management standard covered under [clause 3.0](/risk/what-the-risk-function-produces/); the two cover overlapping enterprise-risk ground and serve as alternatives or complements depending on governance needs.

## Where it goes wrong

**Treating "COSO" as one framework.** The most common error, and the reason this entry exists. "COSO" names a body that publishes at least two frameworks; a policy, RFP answer or control narrative that cites "the COSO framework" without naming Internal Control (2013) or ERM (2017) has not actually said which model it means.

**Reaching for ERM to pass a SOX or SOC 2 audit.** ERM is enterprise risk governance, not a control framework for financial reporting. A team that adopts the 2017 model expecting it to satisfy section 404 or the SOC 2 Common Criteria has taken up the wrong document. The SOC 2 Common Criteria (CC1-CC5) are based on the 2013 internal control framework; SOX section 404 requires a recognised internal-control framework, for which the 2013 framework is the near-universal US choice rather than a statutory mandate.

**Expecting a certificate.** Neither framework is certifiable. There is no accredited COSO certification for an organisation - both are reference models you adopt and assess yourself against, and a vendor badge claiming otherwise is not one.

**Citing the superseded ERM.** The 2017 model replaced an earlier 2004 version built around eight components and a cube diagram. Training and documentation that still describe eight components, or the cube, are describing the superseded framework rather than the current five-component, 20-principle one.

## Common questions

:::faq
### What is the difference between COSO and COSO ERM?

"COSO" is the body, the Committee of Sponsoring Organizations of the Treadway Commission, and it publishes two frameworks. In everyday use "COSO" usually means the Internal Control - Integrated Framework (2013), the internal control model behind SOX section 404 and the SOC 2 Common Criteria. "COSO ERM" means the separate Enterprise Risk Management framework (2017), which governs risk-taking across the enterprise against its strategy. They are different documents answering different questions, so the safe habit is to name the year.

### Is COSO required for SOX compliance?

SOX section 404 requires management to assess internal control over financial reporting against a recognised control framework, and does not name COSO specifically. In practice COSO's Internal Control - Integrated Framework is the near-universal choice among US issuers, to the point that "the framework" in a SOX context is almost always the 2013 COSO model. It is a strong default rather than a legal mandate, and it is not something an organisation is certified against.

### Which COSO framework does SOC 2 use?

The 2013 Internal Control - Integrated Framework. The SOC 2 Common Criteria (CC1-CC5) map to its five components, so a SOC 2 examination rests on COSO ICIF even though the report is issued against the AICPA Trust Services Criteria. COSO ERM (2017) is the enterprise risk framework and is not what SOC 2 is built on.
:::
