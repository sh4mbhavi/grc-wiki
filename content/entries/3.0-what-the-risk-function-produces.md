:::lede
The risk function produces four things: a register of what could go wrong, a
statement of how much of it is acceptable, a plan for the rest, and a log of
what has already happened.
:::

ISO 31000 describes the process — identify, analyse, evaluate, treat, monitor,
communicate — but the artefacts are what other people actually consume, and each
one fails in a recognisable way.

## The four

:::terms
Register :: What could obstruct the objectives, sized, owned and decided. See [3.1](/risk/reading-a-risk-register/). Fails by going stale.
Appetite statement :: How much risk is acceptable, by category, with a threshold. See [3.2](/risk/appetite-vs-tolerance/). Fails by being too abstract to apply.
Treatment plan :: What is being done about everything above the threshold, with owners and dates. See [3.3](/risk/risk-treatment/). Fails by filling with "monitor".
Issue log :: Risks that have occurred, managed to closure rather than on a cycle. Fails by being kept separately from the register, so nobody notices the risk was known.
:::

## How they connect

Governance publishes the appetite. Risk scores the register against it. Anything
above the threshold gets a treatment with an owner and a date; anything below is
monitored on its review cycle. When a risk occurs it moves to the issue log,
keeps its history, and is reviewed on closure to ask whether the score was
wrong.

Break the last link and the function loses the only feedback it gets. A register
that never learns from its own issues is a forecast nobody scores.

## The order to build them

Register first, even a rough one. Twenty entries written badly beat an empty
template, because the argument about the scale is easier to have with real
entries in front of it.

Appetite second, once there is something to apply it to. Writing appetite first
produces a statement calibrated against nothing.

Treatment third. Issue log last, or rather immediately — you will need it the
first week, and it is a table.

:::def term="Risk criteria" ref="3.1"
The written basis for judging significance: the likelihood and impact scales,
the bands, and what each band means. Undocumented criteria produce scores that
cannot be defended or repeated.
:::

## What good looks like after a year

Entries move. Ownership is individual. The top ten are genuinely the top ten
rather than the ten written first. Somebody outside the function can pick an
entry and follow it from raise to treatment to closure without asking a
question.

That last test is the one that matters, and the one most programmes fail.
