:::lede
**NIST SP 800-53** is the US National Institute of Standards and Technology's catalogue of security and privacy controls for information systems and organisations, a control set that other processes draw from, not a framework an organisation is certified against.
:::

The current revision is Revision 5, published in September 2020. It is the control content underneath much of US federal security practice: the Risk Management Framework selects from it, FedRAMP builds its cloud authorisation baselines on it and the [NIST Cybersecurity Framework](/frameworks/nist-csf-2/) (5.5) points at it through its Informative References. What 800-53 does not do is tell you which of its controls apply to you, or issue a certificate when you implement them.

## A catalogue, not a framework

800-53 is a menu. Revision 5 organises the controls into 20 control families, each with a two-letter identifier, and every family holds a set of related controls stated as requirements. The document tells you what a control is; it does not tell you which controls to select, in what order to implement them or whether you have implemented them well. Those decisions live in other documents, which is exactly what makes 800-53 a catalogue rather than a [framework you work through](/frameworks/how-to-read-a-framework/) (5.0).

The families cover the breadth of an information-security programme. A representative sample of the 20:

:::terms
AC, Access Control :: Who and what may reach a system, and on what terms.
AU, Audit and Accountability :: Generating, protecting and reviewing the records that show what happened.
CM, Configuration Management :: Establishing and holding a known-good configuration for systems and components.
IR, Incident Response :: Preparing for, detecting and handling security incidents.
RA, Risk Assessment :: Identifying and analysing the risk that bears on a system.
SC, System and Communications Protection :: Protecting information in transit and the boundaries around it.
PT, PII Processing and Transparency :: Handling personally identifiable information lawfully and openly. Added in Revision 5.
SR, Supply Chain Risk Management :: Managing the risk carried in by suppliers and components. Added in Revision 5.
:::

PT and SR are the two families Revision 5 added; the rest carry over from earlier revisions.

:::def term="Control catalogue" ref="5.0"
A published set of controls an organisation selects and tailors from, rather than a standard an accredited body certifies it against. 800-53 is the large US example; a process such as the Risk Management Framework, or a framework such as the CSF, draws its actual control content from a catalogue like this one.
:::

## What Revision 5 changed

Three changes matter before anything else. Privacy controls were integrated into the main catalogue: the separate privacy appendix of earlier revisions was retired, so security and privacy controls now sit in one consolidated list rather than sending you to a second appendix. Controls were made outcome-based and consolidated: the phrase "the information system" was removed as the implied actor, so a control now applies to whatever entity (a person, a process or a system) is responsible for the outcome, rather than assuming a machine does the work. And the PT and SR families were added, making privacy transparency and supply-chain risk first-class parts of the catalogue.

The fourth change is structural and the one most often missed. The control baselines (the low, moderate and high starting sets) were moved out of 800-53 in Revision 5 into a companion publication, SP 800-53B. In Revision 5 the baselines are not inside 800-53 at all.

## The 800-53 family and the RMF

"800-53" is used loosely to mean three different documents, and the distinction is load-bearing.

| Document | What it is | What it gives you |
| --- | --- | --- |
| SP 800-53 | The control catalogue | The controls themselves |
| SP 800-53A | Assessment procedures | How to assess whether a control is met |
| SP 800-53B | Control baselines | Low, moderate and high starting sets, driven by FIPS 199 |

Impact level comes from FIPS 199, which categorises a system as low, moderate or high; FIPS 200 sets the minimum security requirements; 800-53B turns that categorisation into a starting baseline of controls. All three sit inside the NIST Risk Management Framework, described in SP 800-37 Revision 2, whose seven steps are prepare, categorise, select, implement, assess, authorise and monitor. 800-53 supplies the menu the select step draws from, 800-53B supplies the starting baseline and 800-53A supplies the procedures the assess step runs.

A baseline is a starting point, not a verdict. Controls are then tailored to context (added, removed or adjusted for the system in front of you), and overlays provide ready-made baselines for a specific community or technology so that tailoring does not start from scratch each time.

## Catalogue, shortlist or outcomes

800-53 is one of three documents a team reaches for, and they are not interchangeable. Choosing between them starts with what shape of answer you need.

| Document | Shape | Reach for it to |
| --- | --- | --- |
| NIST SP 800-53 | A large catalogue you select and tailor from | Draw the control content an authorisation or programme needs |
| [CIS Controls](/frameworks/cis-controls/) (5.8) | A prioritised shortlist | Start from a defensible minimum in priority order |
| [NIST CSF 2.0](/frameworks/nist-csf-2/) (5.5) | Outcomes that reference control sets | Structure and communicate a programme; it points at 800-53 for the controls |

The CSF and 800-53 are complementary rather than rival: the framework names an outcome and 800-53 supplies a control that delivers it. The CIS Controls trade comprehensiveness for a running order, which is the opposite bet from 800-53's breadth.

## Where it goes wrong

**Called a certification.** There is no 800-53 certificate for an organisation. What exists is an assessment against the controls (for example the assessment behind an authorisation to operate), not an accredited certification. This is the sharp contrast with ISO 27001, which is certifiable; a supplier claiming to be "800-53 certified" is describing an assessment, and often overstating even that.

**Assuming every control applies.** The catalogue holds far more controls than any one system needs, and treating it as a checklist to satisfy in full misreads it. Baselines and tailoring exist precisely so that most controls do not apply to most systems; the work is selecting and tailoring, not implementing everything.

**Confusing 800-53 with 800-53A or 800-53B.** Since Revision 5 the baselines live in 800-53B and the assessment procedures in 800-53A. Citing "800-53" for a baseline, or for how to assess a control, sends the reader to the wrong document: the catalogue states the controls but not their baselines and not how to test them.

**Treated as mandatory for a private company.** 800-53 is mandatory for US federal information systems, and often for federal contractors, through FISMA. It does not reach a private company with no federal nexus, where it is voluntary, widely adopted and referenced through the CSF Informative References and FedRAMP among others, but not required by FISMA.

## Common questions

:::faq
### Can you get NIST 800-53 certified?
No. There is no 800-53 certification for an organisation and no certificate to hold up. A system can be assessed against the controls (most visibly as part of an authorisation to operate), but that assessment is not an accredited certification in the way ISO/IEC 27001 is. A vendor describing itself as "800-53 certified" is describing an assessment rather than a certificate.

### Is NIST 800-53 mandatory for my company?
It depends on whether you touch the US federal government. FISMA makes 800-53 mandatory for federal information systems and frequently for federal contractors handling government data. A private company with no such nexus is under no FISMA obligation to use it; there it is voluntary, though it is widely adopted and referenced by other frameworks and by FedRAMP.

### What is the difference between 800-53, 800-53A and 800-53B?
800-53 is the control catalogue itself. 800-53A is the companion that gives the procedures for assessing whether a control is met. 800-53B holds the low, moderate and high control baselines, which moved out of 800-53 in Revision 5. Three documents, three jobs: the controls, how to test them and where to start.
:::
