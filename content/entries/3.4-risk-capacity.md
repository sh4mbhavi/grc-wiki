:::lede
Risk capacity is the maximum an organisation could absorb before it becomes
unable to operate. Appetite is set below it on purpose, and the distance between
them is the margin the business is choosing to keep.
:::

Three terms sit on one line and are routinely collapsed into each other:
capacity, appetite, tolerance. Capacity is a fact about the balance sheet and
the licence. Appetite is a choice. Tolerance is a limit on a specific number.

## Measuring capacity

Capacity is not a score. It is expressed in whatever would actually run out.

**Money.** The loss that would breach a covenant, exhaust the cash runway, or
require an emergency raise.

**Time.** The outage after which contracted customers may terminate, which is
usually written in your own service agreements and rarely read by the risk
function.

**Licence.** The regulatory finding that would suspend an authorisation. Binary,
and in regulated sectors it is the binding constraint regardless of the money.

An organisation that has never expressed capacity in at least one of these has
no basis for saying its appetite is set conservatively: it has nothing to be
conservative relative to.

## The gap, and why it is deliberate

Appetite below capacity leaves room for the risks you did not identify. That is
its whole function. A programme that sets appetite at capacity is claiming its
register is complete, which no register is.

How wide the gap should be is a board judgement, not a formula. What is
reasonable to expect is that the board has been shown both numbers on the same
page at least once, and has said the gap is the one it wants.

:::def term="Stress test" ref="3.1"
Applying a plausible severe scenario to the register and asking whether the
combined effect stays inside capacity. The only routine way to find out whether
the gap is real.
:::

## Where it gets used

Rarely in daily work, which is why it goes missing. It matters at exactly three
moments: setting appetite, approving a large acceptance, and after an incident,
when somebody asks how close that came.

The third is when a function that has never articulated capacity discovers it
cannot answer the only question the board wants answered.
