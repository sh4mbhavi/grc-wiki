:::lede
A crosswalk lets one control answer several frameworks at once. Built correctly
it is the reason a second certification costs a fraction of the first; built
incorrectly it is a spreadsheet that has to be thrown away at the next revision.
:::

The critical decision is the shape. Almost every published crosswalk maps
framework A directly to framework B, and almost every useful internal one maps
both through your own control inventory.

## Hub and spoke, not point to point

Direct mapping produces one artefact per pair. Three frameworks means three
mappings; four means six. Each is maintained separately and they drift.

Mapping through your controls means one artefact. Each control lists the
requirements it satisfies across every framework you carry. Adding a fourth
framework adds one column, not three documents.

:::attrs a="Hub and spoke" b="Framework to framework"
Artefacts for 4 frameworks :: +1 :: -6
Adding a framework :: One pass over the inventory :: Three new mappings
Survives a revision :: Re-map one column :: Re-check every pair
Answers "what do we do?" :: +Directly :: -Not at all
Publishable :: Internally :: Sometimes, as marketing
:::

The last row is where the confusion comes from. Vendors publish
framework-to-framework crosswalks because they are marketable. They are a useful
starting reference and a poor operating artefact.

## Building it

Take the control inventory from [4.1](/compliance/mapping-obligations-to-controls/).
For each framework, walk its requirements and record against each control the
requirements it satisfies. Three outputs, as always: mapped, unmapped
requirements, and controls nothing requires.

Two rules keep it honest. A mapping is a claim that this control, as written,
satisfies that requirement. If it only partly does, record it as partial and
name the gap, because a partial mapping recorded as full is exactly what
fieldwork finds. And every mapping cites the requirement identifier, not its
paraphrase, so a revision can be diffed.

## What a revision does to it

ISO 27001 moved from 114 Annex A controls in 2013 to 93 in 2022, restructured
into four themes. Organisations with a hub-and-spoke crosswalk re-mapped one
column. Organisations with three pairwise mappings rebuilt all of them.

Assume a revision every three to five years per framework and build for it.

:::def term="Partial mapping" ref="4.1"
A control that satisfies part of a requirement, recorded with what it does not
cover. The honest form, and the one that stops a gap being discovered during
fieldwork.
:::

## What it actually saves

For a company adding SOC 2 to an existing ISO 27001 programme, the control work
is largely done and the crosswalk shows exactly which parts. What remains is the
system description, the auditor's testing, and the handful of criteria ISO does
not reach.

The saving is not the certificate. It is that the second audit examines a
programme that already runs, rather than a programme assembled to be examined.
