:::lede
A committee exists to make a class of decision that no individual should make
alone. If you cannot name that class, you have scheduled a meeting.
:::

Two artefacts do this work: terms of reference, which say what a forum decides,
and a delegated authority matrix, which says what everybody else may decide
without it.

## Terms of reference, in six lines

Purpose - the class of decision this forum owns. Membership - by role, with a
quorum. Cadence. Inputs - what must be tabled, by whom, how far in advance.
Outputs - minutes, decisions, actions with owners and dates. Escalation - what
this forum sends upward, and on what trigger.

Anything longer is usually a description of the organisation rather than a
mandate. Anything shorter cannot settle an argument about whether a decision was
in scope.

## The delegated authority matrix

One row per decision type, one column per level, and a limit in every cell.

:::attrs a="Written well" b="Written badly"
Decision type :: Accept a residual risk :: Risk decisions
Limit :: Score up to 9 :: As appropriate
Holder :: Named role :: Management
Escalation :: Above 9 to the risk committee :: Escalate if significant
Evidence :: Acceptance recorded in the register :: Minuted
:::

The badly written version is the common one, and it fails in a specific way: it
cannot be applied by the person holding it. "Escalate if significant" means the
holder must guess at the threshold, and every guess is defensible afterwards,
which means nothing is.

## The escalation trigger that actually works

Tie it to the appetite statement. If governance has published *no accepted risk
above a residual score of 12 without board sign-off*, the matrix inherits that
number and the trigger is arithmetic rather than judgement.

Where appetite is qualitative, the trigger has to be categorical instead - any
risk touching customer funds, any obligation with a regulatory penalty attached.
Either works. What does not work is a trigger that requires the person pulling
it to already know the answer.

:::def term="Quorum" ref="2.0"
The minimum attendance for a forum's decisions to stand. Worth setting low
enough that the committee can actually meet, and specific enough that three
people from the same team cannot constitute it.
:::

## How many forums a company actually needs

One, until it does not. A single risk and compliance forum, meeting quarterly,
with the CEO or COO in the chair, covers most organisations under two hundred
people and produces a real minute trail.

Split it when the agenda stops fitting - usually when privacy or a regulator
arrives with its own reporting obligations. Splitting earlier produces forums
that meet without a decision to make, which is how a governance calendar starts
being ignored.
