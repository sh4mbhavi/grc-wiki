:::lede
An audit is an independent examination of evidence against a stated criterion (a
standard, a policy, a regulation, a set of accounts) carried out to form an
opinion that someone who was not there can rely on.
:::

Three words carry the definition. *Independent*: the person forming the opinion
did not design or operate the thing being examined. *Criterion*: there is a
written bar, fixed before the work starts, so the opinion is a comparison and
not a preference. *Opinion*: the output is a judgement about conformity, not a
repair: an auditor who fixes the finding has stopped being one.

An audit is not a review, an assessment or a health check. Those words are used
loosely, but an audit specifically produces assurance: a signed statement, at a
defined level of confidence, that a reader outside the process can act on. That
is why the independence rule is not negotiable. The moment the examiner has a
stake in the answer, the assurance is worth nothing.

## Internal and external audit

The two are often confused because both sample controls and write findings. What
separates them is who they answer to and what they are for.

:::attrs a="Internal audit" b="External audit"
Reports to :: Audit committee of the board :: Shareholders, members or a regulator
Set by :: The organisation, within an approved charter :: Statute, listing rules or a standard
Scope :: Any part of the organisation, risk-ranked :: Fixed: the accounts, or the certified management system
Output :: Findings and recommendations, tracked to closure :: A formal opinion, published or lodged
Frequency :: Continuous programme, annual plan :: Annual, or the certification cycle
Can advise :: +Yes, within limits on self-review :: -No, independence is stricter
:::

Internal audit is the third line in the [three lines model](/foundations/three-lines-model/):
it gives the board assurance that the first line's controls and the second
line's oversight are actually working. External audit sits outside the model
entirely. It serves people outside the organisation who need an independent read
before they rely on its statements.

:::def term="Assurance" ref="1.5"
A stated level of confidence, given by someone independent, that a thing is as
described. Reasonable assurance is a high bar expressed as an opinion; limited
assurance is a lower bar expressed as the absence of anything found.
:::

## The audit cycle

Every audit, internal or external, financial or ISO, runs the same five stages.
The names vary by scheme; the sequence does not.

:::terms
Planning :: Fix the scope, the criterion and the sample. Decide what evidence would be sufficient before asking for any. A scope agreed loosely is renegotiated under pressure later.
Fieldwork :: Gather evidence: documents, system extracts, walk-throughs, re-performance. Test a sample against the criterion and record what was seen, where it came from and when.
Evaluation :: Compare findings to the criterion. Rate each gap by consequence, not by how easy it was to spot. Distinguish an isolated slip from a control that never operated.
Reporting :: State the opinion, then the findings behind it. Each finding names the criterion missed, the evidence, the effect and a recommended owner. The auditee responds with a dated remediation plan.
Follow-up :: Re-test that the agreed actions closed the gap. A finding is open until evidence shows the fix works, not when someone says it is done.
:::

The stage that fails most often is follow-up. Findings are agreed, a management
action plan is filed, and nobody re-tests. The next audit re-raises the same
issue, and the audit function's credibility erodes because its findings turned
out to be optional.

## What auditors actually test

An auditor tests three things about a control, in order, and stops at the first
that fails.

1. **Design.** Would this control, as described, prevent or detect the thing it
   is meant to? A control that cannot work even on paper fails here.
2. **Implementation.** Does the control exist in reality: is it turned on, is
   someone assigned, did it run at all?
3. **Operating effectiveness.** Did it run *every time it should have*, across
   the whole period, on evidence? This is what a SOC 2 Type II or an ISO
   surveillance audit is really checking, and it needs a population, not one
   screenshot.

A finding at the design level is more serious than one at the operating level,
because a redesign is a bigger commitment than tightening execution.

## Common questions

:::faq
### What is the difference between an audit and an assessment?

An audit forms an opinion against a fixed criterion and is carried out by someone
independent of the work. An assessment is usually broader, more advisory, and can
be run by the team that owns the process. An audit produces assurance a third
party can rely on; an assessment produces input the owner can choose to act on.
The words are used interchangeably in marketing, which is why contracts specify
which one is meant.

### What are the main types of audit?

By subject: financial (the accounts), compliance (against a law or regulation),
operational (efficiency and effectiveness of a process), information systems
(controls over technology), and certification (against a standard such as ISO/IEC
27001). By provider: internal, external and second-party, where a customer
audits a supplier directly.

### Do auditors fix the problems they find?

No. An auditor who designs or implements the remediation has audited their own
work and cannot then give an opinion on it: the self-review threat. Auditors
recommend; the auditee owns the fix. Internal auditors may advise on a solution
within limits, but someone else must implement it and a later audit must test it.

### What makes audit evidence sufficient?

Enough of it, relevant to the criterion, and reliable at its source. A system-
generated log is stronger than a manager's assertion; a sample large enough to
represent the population is stronger than one convenient example; evidence dated
inside the audit period is stronger than evidence produced the week of the
audit. Sufficiency is a judgement the auditor has to be able to defend.
:::
