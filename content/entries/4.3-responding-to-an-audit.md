:::lede
An audit is a sampling exercise. The auditor selects which instances of a
control they want to see, you produce them, and the quality of the programme
shows in how few surprises that produces.
:::

The single most useful thing to understand going in: you do not choose the
sample. A programme that has collected its best evidence rather than all of it
discovers this in week one of fieldwork.

## The shape of it

**Planning.** Scope, period, control list, timetable. This is the moment to
correct a misunderstanding about scope, and the last cheap moment to do it.

**Fieldwork.** Requests arrive, usually in batches, usually with a turnaround of
a few days each. Someone must own the queue. Two to four weeks for a first
external audit.

**Findings.** Draft findings come with a right of reply. Read the wording
closely — the difference between "the control was not operating" and "evidence
of operation was not retained" is large and often negotiable on fact.

**Response and closure.** Management response, remediation plan, dates. Then
either closure or carry-forward to the next cycle.

## What to do when the evidence is not there

Say so, immediately, in writing.

The instinct is to reconstruct — regenerate the report today and present it as
though it were produced in March. Auditors are extremely good at spotting this,
it converts a control finding into an integrity finding, and integrity findings
follow a programme for years.

The right move is to state that the instance is missing, say why, say what has
changed so the next period is complete, and let it be a finding. A programme
that self-reports a gap is treated very differently from one caught concealing
the same gap.

## Handling a finding you disagree with

Two kinds of disagreement, handled differently.

**Factual** — the auditor has the control wrong, or has tested the wrong system.
Answer with evidence, not argument, and expect it to be resolved.

**Judgement** — you both agree what happened and disagree about severity. This
is what the management response is for. Say what you accept, say what you
dispute and why, and record it. You will not usually win, and the record still
matters: it is the thing you point at next year when the same finding is raised
against a control you deliberately chose not to change.

:::def term="Sampling" ref="4.2"
Testing a subset of a control's instances to conclude on the whole population.
Because the auditor selects the sample, evidence must be complete rather than
representative.
:::

## The habit that makes audits boring

Run one internally, first, on a handful of controls, with someone who did not
operate them pulling the evidence. It takes two days, finds most of what the
real audit would find, and moves the discovery from fieldwork to a quiet Tuesday.

Programmes that do this describe audits as administrative. Programmes that do
not describe them as the worst month of the year, and both are accurate.
