:::lede
A board has perhaps twelve minutes for your item. The pack that works answers
three questions in that time: what changed, what sits outside appetite, and
what you need the board to decide.
:::

Everything else - the register extract, the control coverage, the audit
timetable - belongs in an annex nobody is expected to read in the room, and
everybody can read afterwards.

## The three questions

**What changed.** Since the last meeting, in the language of the business. Two
new risks above appetite because the payments integration went live; one closed
because the supplier was replaced. Not a delta count.

**What is outside appetite.** The specific entries, their owners, the treatment
and the date. This is the only part of the pack that requires a decision, and it
should be visually obvious which items they are.

**What you need.** A decision, a budget, or an acknowledgement. Say which. A
pack that asks for nothing trains the board to skim it.

## The heat map problem

A four-colour grid with dots on it is the most requested and least useful board
artefact in the discipline. It shows position without showing movement,
compresses away the ownership, and invites an argument about whether something
is amber or red rather than about what to do.

If the board wants one, give them one - and put the movement beside it. An arrow
per entry showing where it was last quarter turns the same picture into a
narrative, which is what a board is actually assembling.

:::def term="Board pack" ref="2.2"
The papers issued in advance of a governing-body meeting. Issued late, it is a
document read in the room; issued a week ahead, it is a document the board has
questions about.
:::

## What a board actually asks

Three questions come up in almost every session, and the pack is stronger if it
pre-empts them.

*Is this getting better or worse?* Answered by trend, not by level.

*What would you do with more money?* Answered by the treatments currently
deferred, with what deferring them costs.

*What are we not seeing?* Answered honestly by naming the parts of the estate
outside scope, which is uncomfortable and builds more credit than anything else
in the pack.

## Cadence

Quarterly, with an out-of-cycle path for anything material. Monthly reporting to
a board produces packs nobody reads and a function that spends its time
reporting rather than working; annual reporting produces surprises.

The out-of-cycle path matters more than the cadence. A board that learns about a
significant incident from the quarterly pack six weeks later will not trust the
quarterly pack again.
