:::lede
A policy waiver, or exception, is a **documented, time-bound decision to run out
of compliance with a control or policy**: an accepted risk with a compensating
control and an expiry date, not a quiet permanent gap. It is the risk acceptance
of [3.3](/risk/risk-treatment/), made explicit.
:::

A waiver does not weaken the policy. It records, in one place and above someone's
signature, that a specific gap is known, is bounded and will be looked at again.
The alternative is not compliance - it is the same gap, undocumented and owned by
nobody.

:::def term="Compensating control" ref="3.3"
An alternative control that reduces the exposure the waived control would have
covered, for the life of the waiver. It does not restore full compliance; it
holds the risk down until the gap is closed or re-decided.
:::

## What a valid waiver carries

A waiver missing any of these is not an exception. It is a gap with a note
attached.

:::terms
The gap being waived :: Which control or policy clause is not being met, stated precisely. "The password policy" is not a gap; "MFA on the legacy billing console" is.
The reason :: Why the exception is needed - a system that cannot meet the control, a delivery deadline, a disproportionate cost - recorded so the next reader can weigh it without asking.
The compensating control :: What holds the exposure down while the gap is open. Named, not promised.
The approver :: One person at a level that matches the size of the risk. A larger exposure is signed higher, by someone who could accept the underlying risk itself.
The expiry date :: The date the waiver ends and the gap returns for a fresh decision.
The review point :: When the exception is checked before that date, in case the reason has gone away sooner.
:::

## Why every waiver expires

A waiver with no end date is not an exception. It is a silent, permanent change
to the policy that nobody approved as permanent. The control still says one
thing; the organisation quietly does another; and the record that this was ever
a decision fades with the person who made it.

The expiry forces the gap back onto a desk for a fresh decision, rather than
letting the exception become the new standard by default. When the date arrives
the choices are three: close the gap; renew the waiver, with the reason and the
compensating control re-examined; or accept the risk permanently through a
documented change to the policy itself. Doing nothing is not one of them. That is
the whole purpose of the date.

## The exception register

Every waiver lives in one register, not in the email that approved it: an
exception register holding every open exception with its expiry, its approver and
its compensating control, in one place that can be counted. It is a sibling of
the issue log the risk function keeps (see
[3.0](/risk/what-the-risk-function-produces/)), not the same book.

Counted and trended, the register says something the individual waivers do not. A
rising number of exceptions against one control is a signal about the control,
not about the teams asking to bypass it: either it is written more tightly than
the organisation can meet, or the policy behind it is wrong. A waiver count that
only ever grows is a policy being repealed one exception at a time, without anyone
deciding to repeal it.

:::faq
### What is the difference between a waiver and risk acceptance?
A waiver is a form of risk acceptance - the acceptance decision from [3.3](/risk/risk-treatment/) - applied to a named control or policy and bounded by an expiry date. Plain acceptance can stand open-ended; a waiver is the same decision made time-bound and tied to a specific gap, so it returns for review rather than lasting forever by omission.

### Who should approve a policy waiver?
One named person at a level that matches the exposure being accepted. A small, well-compensated gap can be signed by the control owner; a material one belongs to the risk owner or an executive - the same person who could accept the underlying risk. The approver must sit high enough to own the consequence if the compensating control fails.

### What happens when a waiver expires but the gap remains?
The exception is out of force the moment it expires, so the organisation is now non-compliant with no decision behind it. Three routes close that: fix the gap; renew the waiver, with the reason and compensating control re-examined; or accept the risk permanently by changing the policy through [2.1](/governance/policy-hierarchy/). An expired waiver left open is the exact failure the expiry date exists to prevent.
:::
